Systems built to remove friction from the parts of life that shouldn't require manual repetition — job search, document filing, market monitoring, and personal finance.

◆

Why This Exists

Four months into an internship I expected to keep, it ended without warning, and the job search that followed made one thing clear: waiting on a broken hiring process to work in your favor isn't a plan. Each of these systems started the same way — a real, specific friction I was living through, automated because doing it by hand once was enough. The job engine exists because cold outreach shouldn't mean a hundred identical emails. Docfiler exists because I spent real hours renaming backdated client files by hand at a wealth management firm, for a problem software should have already solved. The crypto engine exists because a signal worth trusting should be measured, not felt. None of these were built as portfolio pieces first — they were built to be used, and the portfolio is what happens when you keep the receipts.

How These Connect

Underneath four different problems sits one architecture, reused deliberately rather than rebuilt each time: a scheduler that runs continuously instead of waiting to be invoked, a Telegram bot as the control surface for anything that needs a human decision, a local database as the source of truth with everything else — Sheets, dashboards, alerts — as a view onto it, and AI used strictly as a classifier or router, never as the author of anything that ends up in front of another person. The job engine and the crypto engine share the exact same alerting and configuration pattern. Docfiler and the job engine share the same discipline around never letting a model invent a fact it wasn't given. Building the second and third system was faster than the first, because the hard architectural decisions were already made once.
